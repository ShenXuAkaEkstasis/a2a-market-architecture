# A2A Market Architecture

**An open research framework for understanding how commerce changes when AI agents become active market participants.**

**Author:** Shen Xu
**Status:** Research framework v0.1
**Started:** September 2026
**License:** CC BY 4.0

---

## Definition

**A2A Market Architecture** is a research framework for studying how markets, merchants, platforms, channels, and commercial infrastructure change when AI agents participate directly in discovery, evaluation, coordination, negotiation, and transactions.

The term **A2A** here refers broadly to agent-to-agent economic interaction. It is not limited to any single A2A communication protocol.

The central question is:

> **What happens to commerce when AI agents are no longer only interfaces or assistants, but become active participants in markets?**

This project focuses on the architecture of those markets rather than the internal algorithms, model weights, or decision mechanisms of individual AI systems.

---

## Why This Research

Most digital commerce infrastructure was designed around humans.

Websites were designed to be viewed by humans.
Search engines distributed human attention.
Advertising influenced human decisions.
Marketplaces organized human browsing.
Checkout systems assumed a human buyer.
Attribution systems attempted to trace human journeys.

AI agents are beginning to alter these assumptions.

An agent may increasingly:

* discover products or suppliers;
* interpret commercial information;
* compare alternatives;
* coordinate across services;
* request or evaluate offers;
* interact with other agents;
* execute authorized transactions;
* manage post-purchase activities.

This creates a different research problem from traditional e-commerce, search, or digital marketing.

The question is no longer only:

> **How does a business attract human attention?**

It increasingly becomes:

> **How does a business participate in a market in which machines discover, interpret, evaluate, and transact with other machines?**

## Research Scope

A2A Market Architecture studies the commercial layer between AI agents and markets.

The initial research focuses on six areas.

### 1. Market Participants

How do the roles of consumers, buyer agents, seller agents, merchants, platforms, marketplaces, payment providers, identity providers, and infrastructure providers change?

### 2. Interfaces

What replaces or complements the traditional human-facing website when machines become an important commercial audience?

What constitutes an **agent-accessible commercial interface**?

### 3. Discovery and Distribution

How does discovery change when agents mediate demand?

Does commerce move from **traffic allocation** toward **agent-mediated demand allocation**?

What happens to search engines, marketplaces, affiliates, advertising systems, and other distribution intermediaries?

### 4. Commercial State

What information must a merchant expose in machine-accessible form?

Examples may include:

* products and services;
* prices;
* inventory;
* availability;
* delivery conditions;
* geographic restrictions;
* service capabilities;
* contractual terms;
* return policies;
* trust signals;
* transaction capabilities.

The relevant object may increasingly be not simply a web page, but a machine-readable representation of the merchant's current **commercial state**.

### 5. Transactions

What infrastructure is required for agents to move from discovery to execution?

This includes questions involving:

* identity;
* authorization;
* permissions;
* negotiation;
* payments;
* auditability;
* fulfillment;
* refunds;
* disputes.

### 6. Market Economics

How does value distribution change in an agent-mediated market?

Who owns the customer?

Who controls demand?

Who receives attribution?

Where does advertising fit?

What happens to marketplace commissions?

Which intermediaries gain or lose market power?

## Research Questions

The project begins with the following question fan-out.

### Market structure

* Who are the participants in an A2A market?
* What new intermediaries emerge?
* Which existing intermediaries become less important?
* How do buyer agents and seller agents change existing market structures?

### Merchant architecture

* What does an agent-ready merchant look like?
* What information must become machine-readable?
* What must become machine-actionable?
* What is the difference between being visible to an agent and being transactable by an agent?
* Does the merchant need a separate agent interface?

### Discovery and distribution

* How do agents discover merchants?
* How do agents construct candidate sets?
* How does demand allocation differ from traditional traffic allocation?
* What happens to SEO, GEO, marketplaces, advertising, affiliates, and comparison engines?

### Commerce

* How do agents request, compare, negotiate, and accept commercial offers?
* What constitutes a machine-readable offer?
* How should inventory, pricing, availability, and commercial constraints be exposed?
* What infrastructure is required for agent-mediated transactions?

### Trust

* How does an agent verify whether a merchant is legitimate?
* What forms of evidence become machine-verifiable?
* How do identity, reputation, provenance, and authorization interact?

### Economics

* Who owns the customer relationship when an agent mediates the transaction?
* Who captures value when several agents and platforms contribute to one transaction?
* What happens to marketing attribution?
* How might advertising work when an agent rather than a human allocates demand?
* How does A2A commerce change bargaining power among brands, platforms, agents, and infrastructure providers?

## Research Method

This project primarily uses:

* protocol and standards analysis;
* platform and product observation;
* market structure analysis;
* comparative case studies;
* business experiments;
* commercial system mapping;
* longitudinal tracking of emerging agentic commerce infrastructure.

The project does **not** attempt to infer hidden model weights or claim knowledge of proprietary decision algorithms from external outputs.

Where agent decision behavior is discussed, it will be treated separately from claims about internal model mechanisms.

## From AI Visibility to Market Participation

This research builds on a broader transition:

**AI Visibility → Agentic Search → Agent Readiness → Agentic Commerce → A2A Markets**

AI visibility asks whether an AI system can discover and understand an entity.

A2A Market Architecture asks a broader question:

> **Can that entity actually participate in a machine-mediated market?**

Visibility may therefore be only the first layer.

A merchant may ultimately need to become:

**discoverable → interpretable → eligible → transactable → operable**

This transition is one of the central themes of the project.
