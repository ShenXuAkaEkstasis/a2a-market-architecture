# A2A Market Architecture: A Research Agenda for Commerce in an Agent-Mediated Economy

**Shen Xu**
September 2026

## Introduction

Digital commerce was built around a simple assumption: humans discover, evaluate, compare, and buy.

Websites were designed for people to browse. Search engines distributed human attention. Advertising attempted to influence human decisions. Marketplaces organized products for human comparison. Checkout systems assumed that a person would make the final purchase decision.

AI agents are beginning to weaken that assumption.

Agents can already search, compare, summarize, recommend, call tools, retrieve commercial information, and complete parts of transactional workflows. As these systems become more capable, commerce may increasingly involve interactions not only between humans and businesses, but between buyer agents, seller agents, platforms, payment systems, identity providers, and merchant infrastructure.

This creates a research problem that is broader than AI search, GEO, recommendation, or conversational commerce.

The problem is market architecture.

## What Is A2A Market Architecture?

**A2A Market Architecture** is a research framework for studying how markets, merchants, platforms, channels, and commercial infrastructure change when AI agents become active participants in commerce.

The term A2A here refers broadly to agent-to-agent economic interaction. It is not limited to any single communication protocol.

The central research question is:

> **How does commerce change when AI agents participate directly in discovery, evaluation, coordination, negotiation, and transactions?**

This project focuses on commercial systems and market structure.

It does not attempt to infer proprietary model weights, hidden decision trees, or internal ranking mechanisms from external model behavior.

## Why This Research Matters

Much of the current discussion around AI and commerce focuses on individual technologies:

* AI search
* shopping agents
* agent protocols
* payments
* identity
* tool calling
* merchant APIs
* conversational interfaces

These are important, but they are usually discussed separately.

The larger question is how these components interact to create a new commercial environment.

If agents increasingly mediate demand, businesses may need to optimize for more than human attention.

They may need to become:

* discoverable by machines;
* interpretable by machines;
* verifiable by machines;
* comparable by machines;
* callable by machines;
* transactable by machines.

The transition may therefore be larger than a change in interface.

It may represent a shift from **human-mediated digital commerce** toward **agent-mediated markets**.

## From Human Interfaces to Agent Interfaces

For most of the internet era, businesses optimized the human interface.

They improved:

* websites;
* landing pages;
* product pages;
* search visibility;
* advertising;
* conversion funnels;
* checkout experiences.

In an agent-mediated market, a second interface may become increasingly important:

**the agent interface.**

An agent does not necessarily need the same information architecture as a human.

It may require direct access to:

* structured product information;
* pricing;
* inventory;
* availability;
* delivery conditions;
* service capabilities;
* geographic restrictions;
* contractual terms;
* return policies;
* trust evidence;
* identity information;
* payment capabilities;
* machine-actionable workflows.

This raises a basic question:

> **What does a business need to expose when the commercial audience is not only human?**

## From Traffic Allocation to Demand Allocation

Traditional digital commerce is heavily organized around traffic.

Search engines, social platforms, affiliates, marketplaces, and advertising networks direct people toward websites, stores, or product pages.

AI agents may create a different distribution model.

Instead of allocating traffic, an agent may allocate demand.

A user may express an intention once, while the agent performs discovery, comparison, filtering, coordination, and potentially transaction execution.

If this becomes common, the economic role of search engines, marketplaces, advertising platforms, and merchant websites may change.

The relevant question becomes:

> **Who controls demand when agents mediate the path between intent and transaction?**

## From Web Pages to Commercial State

A web page is a representation of a business at a moment in time.

But an agent may need something more operational.

It may need access to the merchant's current commercial state.

This could include:

* what is available;
* at what price;
* under what conditions;
* where it can be delivered;
* when it can be fulfilled;
* whether the merchant is eligible for a specific transaction;
* what terms apply;
* what actions can be executed.

In this environment, the important unit may no longer be only the page.

It may increasingly be the machine-accessible representation of the merchant's **commercial state**.

## From Visibility to Market Participation

AI visibility asks whether an AI system can discover and understand an entity.

That is important, but it may only represent the first stage.

A business can be visible to an agent without being usable by that agent.

A more complete progression may be:

**Discoverable → Interpretable → Eligible → Transactable → Operable**

This suggests a distinction between:

**AI visibility**

and

**A2A market participation.**

The first concerns whether an entity can be found and understood.

The second concerns whether that entity can actually participate in machine-mediated commerce.

## Research Questions

This project will explore a growing set of questions.

### Market Structure

* Who are the participants in an A2A market?
* How do buyer agents and seller agents change existing market structures?
* Which intermediaries gain importance?
* Which intermediaries lose importance?
* What new commercial roles emerge?

### Merchant Architecture

* What does an agent-ready merchant look like?
* What commercial information must become machine-readable?
* What must become machine-actionable?
* Does a merchant need a dedicated agent interface?
* What is the difference between a visible merchant and a transactable merchant?

### Discovery and Distribution

* How do agents discover merchants?
* How does agent-mediated demand allocation differ from traffic allocation?
* What happens to SEO, GEO, marketplaces, advertising, affiliates, and comparison platforms?
* Does the agent become a new distribution layer?

### Commercial State

* What information must merchants expose dynamically?
* How should price, inventory, availability, service capability, and transaction conditions be represented?
* What is the machine-readable equivalent of a commercial offer?

### Transactions

* What infrastructure is required for agent-mediated transactions?
* How do identity, authorization, permissions, payments, fulfillment, and dispute handling interact?
* What changes when agents can move from recommendation to execution?

### Trust

* How does an agent verify whether a merchant is legitimate?
* What forms of reputation and evidence are machine-verifiable?
* How do identity, provenance, authorization, and trust interact?

### Market Economics

* Who owns the customer relationship?
* Who captures value in a multi-agent transaction?
* What happens to attribution?
* How might advertising work when agents allocate demand?
* What happens to marketplace commissions?
* How is bargaining power redistributed among brands, agents, platforms, and infrastructure providers?

## Research Method

This project will primarily use:

* protocol and standards analysis;
* platform and product observation;
* market structure analysis;
* comparative case studies;
* business experiments;
* commercial system mapping;
* longitudinal tracking of emerging agentic commerce infrastructure.

The goal is not to reverse-engineer proprietary model internals.

The goal is to understand how commercial systems change as agents become increasingly capable of participating in markets.

## Research Direction

The initial research program will focus on several connected themes:

* A2A Market Architecture
* Agent Interfaces
* Agent-Mediated Demand Allocation
* Machine-Readable Commercial State
* Agentic Merchandising
* Merchant Readiness
* AI Visibility vs. Market Participation
* Attribution Collapse in A2A Markets
* Buyer Agents and Seller Agents
* A2A Market Economics

These topics will be developed as separate research notes and linked back to this research agenda.

## Closing Question

The internet economy spent decades optimizing how businesses present themselves to humans.

The next phase may require a different question:

> **What does a business need to become when machines can discover, evaluate, coordinate with, and transact with it directly?**

That is the research space this project will explore.
